Bangla Digraphs

Pronounciation guide

The characters between the //s are a pronunciation guide written using the International Phonetic Alphabet. If you're unfamiliar, here's a quick guide:

  • Letters you recognize (k, t, m, n, d, h) are mostly pronounced as you'd expect.
  • j is pronounced like y as a consonant, as in "yellow."
  • ŋ is pronounced like ng as in "sing" but try to make it one sound.
  • ɳ is pronounced like ni (ny) as in "onion" or ñ in Spanish.
  • ʃ is pronounced like sh as in "shout".
  • ɾ is pronounced like tt as in "bottle" or "butter" as said by someone in a rush.
  • gʲ is pronounced like gy as in, let's say, the middle of "hog yard."
  • d͡z is pronounced like a 'hard' j as in "jump."
  • tʃ͡ is pronounced like ch as in "chow."
  • ː means "longer;" /kː/ should be "longer" (or more pronounced) than a "regular" /k/.
  • ʰ means "aspirated." TBQH it seems to me that the degree of aspiration might vary. In particular, ভ /bʰ/ is sometimes (mostly?) be pronounced /v/ (as in ভোট, /vot/ "vote") and ফ is sometimes (mostly?) pronounced /f/ (as in ফুচকা, "fuchka.") By contrast, খ is definitely /kʰ/ and not e.g. /x/ (the Scottish/German ch as in "loch") and থ is definitely /tʰ/ not e.g. /θ/ (th as in "thin")

Other notes

I am not a native speaker of Bangla. This guide is the product of my personal effort. It should not be construed as rigorous or definitive, although I believe it's at least largely correct. In particular:

  • there are almost assuredly variant pronunciations of some of these among and within dialects
  • I'm treating the retroflex consonants (ট, ঠ, ড, ঢ, ণ) "non-retroflexively," as I'm not convinced the distinction is actually made in everyday Bangla
  • I may have misunderstood the exact details of the bôphôla
If you are a Bangla speaker and want to correct my pronounciations, PLEASE @ me on Bluesky or send me an email at sam dot raker at gmail dot com.
